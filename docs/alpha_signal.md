# Alpha-signal ablation (plan.md Phase 3, minimal)

Generated by `scripts/step5_alpha_signal.py`. Walk-forward (expanding window, chronological, 5 folds, 20% warm-up) ridge regression on AAPL 2024-06-03, 23400 one-second rows. One day, one instrument -- treat as a first pass, not a validated edge.

**Question:** does the Hawkes order-flow signal (`hawkes_ofi`, `lambda_*_hawkes`) forecast future price moves or future order flow better than the EWMA baseline already in the feature table (`order_flow_imbalance`, `lambda_*_ewm`)? Two models are compared at each target: `baseline_ewma` (9 microstructure/EWMA features) vs `with_hawkes` (same 9 plus the 3 Hawkes features). Both are plain ridge (alpha=1, not tuned) on standardized features; no look-ahead: train-fold mean/std only, each fold trains strictly on rows before its test block, and the Hawkes parameters are re-estimated on each fold's training window (fitting them on the whole day, as step 2 does, would hand the Hawkes features information from the test folds that the parameter-free EWMA does not get).

## Results

OOS R^2 is against the "predict zero" benchmark (standard for short-horizon return forecasting, since the unconditional mean return is ~0). Hit rate is scored on rows whose target is non-zero (about half of AAPL's 1 s returns are exactly zero at a 1-cent tick, and no sign call can hit those). Economic value is mean(sign(prediction) * realized return) per row, trading every row; "net" subtracts a half-spread crossing cost. Trading every second at the spread is not a strategy anyone would run, so net PnL is dominated by cost for both models; read it as a relative comparison only. It is not defined for the order-flow target. CIs are a t-interval over the folds actually used (5 per target) -- small n, treat the width as a floor on the uncertainty, not the whole of it (one day's worth of folds is not independent data).

## Paired significance test

Comparing two overlapping confidence intervals is a weak test (it is biased toward "no difference"). This tests the per-row squared-error difference directly: both models are fit on identical rows and identical folds, so `se_diff = (baseline error)^2 - (hawkes error)^2` is a paired observation -- positive means Hawkes had the lower error on that row. Two versions are reported: **fold-level** (one mean per fold, t-test with n = folds -- conservative, few degrees of freedom) and **Diebold-Mariano** (all OOS rows pooled, with a Newey-West long-run variance, Bartlett lag = horizon + 10). A plain pooled t-test would be badly over-confident here: adjacent 1 s rows are serially correlated and the 5 s / 10 s targets overlap by construction.

| Target | Mean se_diff (fold, 95% CI) | Fold t-test p | Diebold-Mariano (HAC) p | n pooled rows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `future_ret_1s` | -2.484e-12 [-1.390e-11, 8.933e-12] | 0.578 | 0.418 | 18672 | null |
| `future_ret_5s` | -7.694e-11 [-3.153e-10, 1.614e-10] | 0.421 | 0.170 | 18668 | null |
| `future_ret_10s` | -4.837e-10 [-1.301e-09, 3.332e-10] | 0.176 | 0.019 | 18664 | suggestive (EWMA better) |
| `future_ofi_1s` | 5.014e-04 [-3.081e-03, 4.083e-03] | 0.717 | 0.440 | 12224 | null |

Four targets are tested, so the Bonferroni threshold for a family-wise 5% level is p < 0.0125. **robust** = both tests clear it; **suggestive** = at least one test is below 0.05; **null** otherwise. A negative mean means the Hawkes features made the forecast *worse* (extra, noisier regressors).

## Reading this table

If `with_hawkes`'s CI does not clearly separate from `baseline_ewma`'s on OOS R^2 and net PnL, **and** the paired test above does not reject zero, the Hawkes features are not shown to add predictive value beyond the EWMA baseline on this day. That is consistent with the corrected engine comparison (`output/step4b_fixed/step4b_report.txt`), where Hawkes and baseline quoting are statistically indistinguishable once fills are replayed from real trades. A result that only shows up in the high-toxicity regime would be a materially different finding from a flat null, and is what the regime table above is for.

## Event-time multi-scale features

The discrete Hawkes model sees only 1 s counts, but the exact fit in `docs/hawkes_calibration.md` puts most of the excitation at millisecond scale. This model replaces the three Hawkes features with exponentially decayed counts of buy and sell aggressor events at half-lives of 0.1 s, 1 s, 10 s, computed from the individual trade timestamps and sampled at each bucket's end (log intensity per side, plus an imbalance, per scale). A linear model on these is a learned sum-of-exponentials Hawkes intensity. Same baseline, folds and tests as above.

## Literature check: is an HJB + Hawkes optimal-control reformulation the next step?

Checked against Jain, Firoozye, Kochems & Treleaven, *"An Impulse Control Approach to Market Making in a Hawkes LOB Market"* (arXiv:2510.26438v2, Oct 2025), the most directly relevant recent paper attempting exactly this. Findings relevant here, read directly from the paper rather than taken from its abstract:

- They write the HJB quasi-variational inequality for a Hawkes-driven market maker, then state a closed-form solution is infeasible. Their numerical PDE solver (Deep Galerkin) does not converge for the full multivariate Hawkes model; their headline result instead comes from a PPO reinforcement-learning agent trained in their own simulator.
- No real market data is used anywhere in the paper -- validation is against their own simulator's stylized facts, not a historical dataset.
- No comparison against Avellaneda-Stoikov or any standard heuristic quoting strategy is reported -- only against their own rule-based baseline.
- No confidence intervals, seed counts, or significance tests accompany the headline Sharpe ratios.

Conclusion: a validated, closed-form HJB+Hawkes reformulation is not a two-day task -- it was not a converged result for a funded team attempting exactly this. The more defensible use of limited time is the direct empirical test above: does the signal predict anything, checked ourselves, rather than assumed from a paper's framing. An HJB/RL reformulation remains the right next step (plan.md Phase 6a) **conditional on** this ablation showing the Hawkes signal adds out-of-sample value; optimizing control of a signal not shown to predict anything would not be a meaningful next step.

## Limitations

- One day, one instrument -- folds are time-adjacent, not independent; CIs understate true uncertainty.
- Ridge alpha=1.0 is fixed, not cross-validated.
- The economic-value cost assumption (half the quoted spread) is a simplification, not a fitted fill model; it is not the step 3 engine's fill probabilities.
- `future_ofi_1s` is a realized one-second-ahead order-flow imbalance, not a tradeable quantity by itself; it isolates whether Hawkes intensities forecast flow, separately from price.
